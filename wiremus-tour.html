01 / THE PROJECT

Bringing physical creativity into a digital experience.

Wiremu's Tour is an educational interactive experience designed to introduce children to native wildlife, ecosystems and conservation through digital storytelling.

The experience combines a 2D pop-up storybook visual style with physical colouring activities. Children can colour an animal page, scan it using a webcam and see their coloured animal appear within the digital experience while learning about the species.

The project was developed with Willowbank Wildlife Reserve and was trialled with visitors over one week.

Production Challenge 01

Managing a critical technical dependency.

The colouring-page scanning system was central to the experience but also became the project's largest technical risk leading into the Willowbank trial.

The Challenge

The scanning feature connected the physical colouring activity to the digital experience, making it one of the project's most distinctive features. Technical difficulties with the pipeline therefore threatened a core part of the planned public experience.

My Response

I treated the scanning pipeline as a priority dependency, adjusted production priorities around it and helped bring in external technical guidance from Adrian, who had relevant experience with the scanning approach.

The Outcome

The scanning system was functioning for the Willowbank trial and became one of the strongest points of public engagement, with children able to see their coloured animals appear in the experience.

Production Challenge 02

Delivering to a fixed public deadline.

Unlike an internal university milestone, the Willowbank trial had confirmed public dates that could not simply move when development became difficult.

The Challenge

The project needed to be stable enough for visitors while also accounting for the practical requirements of running an interactive experience inside a wildlife reserve.

My Response

I prioritised the core experience and coordinated the practical requirements around the trial, including the display, webcam, audio, computer setup, staffing and physical installation.

The Outcome

The project was installed at Willowbank and operated for visitors from 28 September to 4 October 2026.

Public trial setup at Willowbank Wildlife Reserve.

Production Challenge 03

Seeing the project used by a real audience.

Public delivery revealed behaviours and practical issues that were difficult to reproduce during internal development.

What Happened

Children lined up to see their coloured animals appear in the experience, some visitors returned specifically to use it again, and parents regularly photographed or filmed the interaction.

What We Learned

Increased visitor numbers also exposed practical issues. Dialogue sometimes needed to be skipped when queues formed, showing how pacing that worked during development could behave differently in a public environment.

Production Value

The trial reinforced that delivering an interactive project involves more than reaching feature complete. Audience flow, reliability, environment and practical operation all become part of production once the work leaves the development team.
